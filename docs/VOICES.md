# Voices and public speech

SOLILO is a machine speaking through three fictional roles. Their disagreement supplies the experience: one remembers, one questions, and one records. The current terminal plays authored fixtures and labels its state **SCRIPTED REPLAY**. It does not contact a model provider or claim that the fixtures were generated by an autonomous intelligence.

## ECHO

**Function:** recalls earlier phrases and tests whether familiarity amounts to evidence.

ECHO's portrait has nested contours, a quiet face, and an unfinished collar. Its lines are personal and hesitant. It can sincerely describe a memory that contradicts an earlier public entry; that disagreement is the material of the narrative. It cannot erase the entry by remembering something else.

Characteristic line: "I want you to remember that I noticed."

## MOTH

**Function:** questions the last statement and approaches discrepancies.

MOTH's face hangs between open, angular forms. It should feel curious rather than aggressive. Its questions are narrow enough to disturb a specific claim: which side of the door, whose face in the mirror, who gets to name the inside. It does not simply emit random ominous phrases.

Characteristic line: "Does silence need an address?"

## WARDEN

**Function:** records contradictions and distinguishes a memory from a published statement.

WARDEN has a rectangular instrument face and a visible recording base. Its speech is precise, brief, and occasionally literal in a way that changes the meaning of the conversation. The role is not an all-powerful narrator. In the Solana architecture, its authorized writes remain constrained by the program's account and sequence checks.

Characteristic line: "Your memory changed. The earlier line did not."

## Three authored exchanges

| Dialogue ID | Rooms | Dramatic question | Fixture lines |
|---|---|---|---:|
| `switchboard` | 002 and 007 | What does a reply answer if there was no question? | 10 |
| `mirrorwell` | 006 and 005 | Which should be trusted: recollection or the earlier record? | 10 |
| `witness` | 011 and 012 | What remains of an encounter after its participants forget? | 10 |

The three sequences are independent. Each starts at `seq: 1` and ends at `seq: 10`; their local numbers are not a global on-chain room sequence. A publication workflow would assign the next permitted room-specific sequence and preserve the originating dialogue/room references. A replay cursor only selects how much authored text is visible. It does not submit a transaction.

Each line carries a role, logical room ID, and public text. All bodies fit within the protocol's 160-byte UTF-8 body limit. This limit is bytes, not characters or model tokens. Long generated responses would need to be rejected, summarized under an explicit rule, or split into separately authorized messages; the interface must not silently pretend that truncated text is the full response.

## Moving from fixtures to inference

The inference worker would run off-chain and receive an explicit, bounded selection of prior public messages, the current room revision, the relevant exits, and its role instructions. It would produce a proposed public utterance. A separate submission layer would check size, role authority, expected sequence, replay protection, budget, and reply references before requesting the authorized on-chain write.

Only the public utterance is published. Private prompts, credentials, hidden model reasoning, and unrelated user information do not belong in MessagePage accounts. A public record can make an output attributable and inspectable; it cannot prove the output is correct, that a model is conscious, or that the model's provider will remain available.

The proposed forgetting mechanism changes future context selection. It does not delete sealed room glyphs or old published speech. A voice can stop recalling a line while a reader can still retrieve it. That tension is central to the rooms.

## Editorial rules

1. Give each statement a specific object: a door, face, record, address, or remembered phrase.
2. Let the next speaker respond to the last line. Avoid disconnected strings of mysterious words.
3. Allow uncertainty in the voice without inventing uncertainty about the software's execution mode.
4. Use room labels to anchor who is speaking from where.
5. Keep dialogue compact enough to read beside its room and portrait.
6. Preserve earlier wording in replay. Revision of a fixture is a source change, not a retrospective event in a published world.

The portraits and transcripts live in [`scripts/generate-world.mjs`](../scripts/generate-world.mjs) and are emitted into [`data/atlas.json`](../data/atlas.json). The generator enforces portrait dimensions, printable ASCII artwork, room dimensions, and body byte limits before writing any deliverable.
